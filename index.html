Unequal Heat Vulnerability in Philadelphia

Climate change is making Philadelphia hotter and increasing the danger of extreme heat. But heat risk is not shared equally across the city.

This story map moves from the citywide pattern of heat vulnerability to Hunting Park, where heat exposure, social sensitivity, parks, and street trees can be viewed together.

Heat vulnerability is not evenly distributed

Philadelphia's Heat Vulnerability Index (HVI) combines heat exposure with the social and health factors that make residents more sensitive to extreme heat. Higher values indicate greater combined vulnerability.

A closer look at Hunting Park Area

Zooming in reveals several high-vulnerability census tracts in and around Hunting Park. The pattern reflects both hotter local conditions and a greater sensitivity to heat-related health risks.

Heat vulnerability, parks, and trees

Within the same area, this map layers PPR-managed parks and the street-tree inventory over heat vulnerability. It shows a spatial relationship, not proof that park or tree access alone causes heat vulnerability.

Sources: Heat Vulnerability by Census Tract (2023), PPR Properties, and Philadelphia Tree Inventory (2025).
